# Professional Learning - Supplementary Notes

## 1. Instructional Framework - TPACK

I designed a laser engraving activity for 9th graders centered around a practical goal: helping students transform their own creative ideas into designs that could actually be engraved on wood. Using AI prompts, students propose their own unique ideas and requirements for their designs, evaluate whether the generated images meet these requirements, and decide how to improve them. They also consider the limitations of laser engraving, where clear outlines, a simple background, and just the right amount of detail are more important than mere visual appeal. Within the TPACK framework, this lesson integrates technological knowledge, content knowledge, and pedagogical knowledge (Marino et al., 2009). This integration supports purposeful digital design and problem-solving within Design and Technology education (ULO2).

## 2. Inclusive Practices - Universal Design for Learning (UDL)

Not every student can easily express ideas through drawing, so I provided multiple pathways: sketching, creating or editing in Canva, or using school-approved AI tools to help visualize original concepts. Students may also choose not to use AI. They can create, test, and refine their design ideas in whatever way works best for them. Offering multiple ways to express ideas helps enhance learner autonomy and aligns with the UDL principle of multiple modes of action and expression (CAST, 2024). These flexible learning pathways support inclusive participation and allow students with different abilities and learning needs to express their creativity (ULO3).

## 3. Digital Safety and Ethics

Because biases exist in AI systems and the images they generate (Ferrara, 2024; Zhou et al., 2024), before students use AI, I will show them examples of AI-generated images that are biased or misleading and ask them to first identify the problems in these images. Subsequently, based on their observations, I will explain why it is necessary to critically evaluate the generated results. Students will also discuss copyright and attribution issues, avoid entering personal information, and use school-approved tools. These safeguards reflect the requirements of the *Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools*, particularly regarding critical thinking, academic integrity, privacy, and copyright (Government of Australia, 2023), as well as the emphasis placed by eSafety on digital citizenship education (eSafety Commissioner, 2021). These practices promote ethical and responsible AI use while developing students' critical awareness of digital technologies (ULO1).

## 4. Evidence of Learning and Unit Learning Outcomes

Students will document their initial ideas, successful and unsuccessful prompts, issues they encountered, and the revisions they made. I will assess the improvements students made through the iterative process, while also evaluating whether the images are clearly presented on the board and reflect the students' ideas. Students will demonstrate how they made design decisions through brief descriptions written in their own words.

The activity also aligns with the Australian Curriculum content descriptions AC9TDE10P02 and AC9TDE10K01.
